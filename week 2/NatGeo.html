National Geographic's Vintage Photos of a Young Dolly Parton

Dolly Parton’s larger-than-life career began with humble roots in Tennessee. Photographer Jodi Cobb was there to capture her early stardom.

By Domonique Tolliver

Published August 25, 2026

  • Facebook
  • X
  • Email
  • Copy link

Dolly Parton—whose unmistakable voice and extraordinary songwriting made her one of the most recognizable figures in American culture—has died at 80 years old, as confirmed in a video message from her family.

For more than six decades, the “Queen of Country” wove stories of heartbreak, ambition, and home into songs shared across generations. Her songwriting formed a tapestry of the beauty and hardships of life in Appalachia, where she spent her childhood before pursuing her music career in Nashville.

Parton was a philanthropic force. She donated hundreds of millions of dollars over her lifetime and was recognized with the Carnegie Medal of Philanthropy in 2022. Her work in literacy is a defining aspect of her legacy, connecting millions of children with free books through her Imagination Library and giving the gift of education through her Dollywood Foundation. She also gave $1 million to fund COVID-19 research and supported the development of the Moderna mRNA vaccine.

Parton also did her part in protecting our planet. Raised among the Great Smoky Mountains of East Tennessee, Parton was vocal about her love for the state and the importance of protecting our natural resources. In a 2022 interview with National Geographic, Parton said, “mistreating Mother Nature” is like "being ugly to your mama.”

National Geographic photographer Jodi Cobb captured Parton in her first decade of stardom for our May 1978 feature story on Nashville’s vibrant culture. Here are some of those archival images—including several that have never been published before.
